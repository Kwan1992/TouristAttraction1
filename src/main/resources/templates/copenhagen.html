Welcome to Copenhagen

Royal palaces, colourful canals and plenty to do with kids. Start here.

Tivoli

Tivoli Gardens is a historic amusement park and pleasure garden in Copenhagen, Denmark. Opened in 1843, it is the second-oldest operating amusement park in the world and one of the most visited attractions in the country, known for its beautiful gardens, thrilling rides, and open-air concerts.

Little Mermaid

The Little Mermaid (Danish: Den lille Havfrue) is a bronze statue by Edvard Eriksen, depicting a mermaid becoming human. The sculpture is displayed on a rock by the waterside at the Langelinie promenade in Copenhagen, Denmark. It is 1.25 meters (4.1 ft) tall and weighs 175 kilograms (385 lb).

Rosenborg Castle

Rosenborg Castle is a Renaissance-style castle located in the heart of Copenhagen, Denmark. Built in the early 1600s by King Christian IV as a summer residence, it is one of the country's most cherished royal buildings, surrounded by the lush King's Garden.

Frederiksborg Castle

Frederiksborg Castle is a grand Baroque-style palace built in the early 17th century by King Christian IV, located on three islets in the town of Hillerød, Denmark. Today it houses the Museum of National History, showcasing centuries of Danish art and history within its ornate halls and surrounding gardens.

Visit website ↗

Nyhavn

Nyhavn is a picturesque 17th-century waterfront, canal, and entertainment district in Copenhagen, Denmark, lined with colorful gabled townhouses, bars, and restaurants. Once a bustling commercial port, it is now one of the city's most iconic and photographed spots, popular for its lively atmosphere and historic wooden ships.

The National Aquarium Denmark

Den Blå Planet The National Aquarium Denmark is Northern Europe's largest aquarium, offering a round-the-world journey under the sea for children and adults alike. Experience thousands of fish and sea creatures up close in 48 aquariums containing a total of 7 million liters of water.

Visit website ↗

The Round Tower

The Round Tower (Danish: Rundetårn) is a 17th-century tower in Copenhagen, Denmark, one of the many architectural projects of Christian IV. Built as an astronomical observatory, it is noted for its equestrian staircase, a 7.5-turn helical corridor leading to the platform at the top (34.8 meters above ground), and its views over Copenhagen.

Copenhagen Zoo

Copenhagen Zoo is one of the oldest zoos in Europe. It opened in 1859 in Frederiksberg, Copenhagen. The zoo was created to give people the opportunity to see and learn about animals from different parts of the world. Today, it is still a popular attraction and focuses on animals, nature and conservation.

Christiansborg Palace

Christiansborg Palace on Slotsholmen is the only building in the world housing all three branches of government at once: the Danish Parliament, the Supreme Court, and the Prime Minister's Office, alongside the Royal Reception Rooms still used for state events.

Visit website ↗

Amalienborg Palace

Winter home of the Danish royal family, the Amalienborg Palace complex consists of four nearly identical Rococo palace buildings arranged symmetrically around a spacious octagonal courtyard. At its center stands an equestrian statue of King Frederik V. Each of the four palaces has served as residence to different members of the royal family over the centuries, and together they remain the King's official winter residence today, alongside select rooms open to the public as museum spaces.
